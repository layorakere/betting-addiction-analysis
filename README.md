# Betting Addiction Among Nigerians

## Project Overview

This project explores betting behaviour among a sample of 2,000 records, using Microsoft Excel to compare average weekly betting activity and addiction scores across age groups, betting platforms, and locations.

I organised the analysis using PivotTables and developed an interactive dashboard to present key indicators and compare patterns across the selected categories. The dashboard includes filters for age group and betting platform, alongside visualisations of average weekly bets and addiction scores by platform, age group, and region.

The project focuses on describing differences in the recorded data and making those patterns easier to explore through visualisation. The findings are interpreted as descriptive observations rather than proof that any particular factor causes betting addiction.

## Business Problem

Betting behaviour can vary across age groups, betting platforms, and locations. Analysing these differences can help make patterns in betting activity and recorded addiction scores easier to identify and explore.

This project uses a sample of 2,000 records to examine average weekly betting activity and addiction scores across these categories. An interactive Excel dashboard presents the results in a format that allows users to compare the recorded values.

## Project Objectives

- Examine average weekly betting activity across betting platforms.
- Compare average addiction scores across betting platforms.
- Analyse differences in average addiction scores and weekly betting activity across age groups.
- Compare average weekly betting activity and addiction scores across locations.
- Develop an interactive dashboard with filters for age group and betting platform.
- Present the results through visualisations that support exploration of the recorded data.

## Tools and Technologies

- **Microsoft Excel:** Used to organise the analysis and build the dashboard.
- **Excel PivotTables:** Used to summarise and compare average weekly betting activity and addiction scores across betting platforms, age groups, and locations.
- **Excel Dashboard and Slicers:** Used to present the results visually and allow users to filter the analysis by age group and betting platform.

## Dataset Overview

The dataset contains **2,000 records** and 17 columns relating to betting behaviour among Nigerians.

The analysis examines recorded betting activity and addiction scores across three main dimensions:

- **Age group:** Youth (18–25), Adult (26–40), and Old (41–60).
- **Betting platform:** Bet9ja, Betway, BetKing, SportyBet, 1xBet, and NairaBet.
- **Location:** Lagos, Abuja, Ibadan, Kano, Enugu, and Port Harcourt.

The dashboard focuses on average weekly betting activity and average addiction scores, allowing comparisons across these categories.

## Data Preparation

The dataset was used for analysis in Microsoft Excel. I reviewed the available fields and organised the data using PivotTables to summarise betting activity and addiction scores across the selected categories.

The analysis focused on comparing average weekly betting activity and average addiction scores by betting platform, age group, and location. These summaries were then used to build the dashboard visualisations and support interactive filtering.

No Power Query transformations were performed as part of this project.

## Key Findings

- **Betting platform differences:** Bet9ja recorded the highest average addiction score (5.68), while SportyBet recorded the lowest (5.31).
- **Age group differences:** The Adult group (26–40) had the highest average addiction score (5.62), while the Old group (41–60) had the lowest (5.43).
- **Weekly betting activity by age:** The Youth group (18–25) recorded the highest average weekly betting activity at 13.50, compared with 13.09 for Adults and 12.79 for the Old group.
- **Location differences in addiction scores:** Ibadan had the highest average addiction score (5.72), closely followed by Lagos (5.70). Kano had the lowest score (5.35).
- **Location differences in weekly betting activity:** Enugu recorded the highest average weekly betting activity (13.50), while Port Harcourt recorded the lowest (12.29).
- **Overall indicators:** Across the 2,000 records, the average weekly betting figure was 13.13 and the average addiction score was 5.54.

These findings describe differences in the recorded averages. They do not establish the causes of betting behaviour or demonstrate that any group or platform has a higher clinical rate of addiction.

## Recommendations

Based on the patterns observed in the dashboard, the following areas may warrant further investigation:

- **Investigate platform-level differences:** Examine whether differences in average addiction scores across betting platforms persist when other relevant factors are considered.
- **Explore age-group patterns:** Further analyse the relationship between age groups, weekly betting activity, and addiction scores to understand the differences in the recorded averages.
- **Examine location-level variations:** Investigate the factors that may contribute to the differences in average weekly betting activity and addiction scores across locations.
- **Support responsible betting initiatives:** Use the findings as a starting point for exploring responsible betting awareness and support initiatives, without labelling any group or location as inherently more addicted.
- **Extend the analysis:** Incorporate additional relevant variables and, where available, information on sample selection to provide more context for interpreting the results.

## Limitations and Considerations

- **Descriptive analysis:** The dashboard presents averages and comparisons. It does not establish causal relationships between betting platforms, age groups, locations, and addiction scores.
- **Interpretation of addiction scores:** The displayed scores should be interpreted according to how the source dataset defines and measures them. They should not be treated as clinical diagnoses without appropriate validation.
- **Sample representativeness:** Although the dataset contains 2,000 records, the extent to which the sample represents the wider Nigerian population has not been established.
- **Limited context:** The dashboard focuses on average weekly betting activity and addiction scores. Additional variables may be needed to understand the differences observed.
- **Scope of analysis:** The project focuses on descriptive analysis and dashboard visualisation rather than statistical testing or predictive modelling.
